Title: AI for better team efficiency and business alignment
The Task(s) and Context
Our business-facing data science team owns the full analytical lifecycle for the derivatives business — framing a business question with trading and product stakeholders, building the analysis, and shipping production tooling on Google Cloud. The work spans two areas. First, market microstructure and liquidity studies: order book depth, spread dynamics, and fill quality, built in Python notebooks against BigQuery. Second, digital analytics for our client-facing web properties, where we study how clients navigate our web pages, tools, and educational videos before they trade a product — so we can recommend the right content, surface the right product, and drive cross-sell and up-sell. The friction was rarely the analysis itself; it was the surrounding software lifecycle. Analysts spent large portions of every sprint hand-writing repetitive BigQuery SQL, translating vague stakeholder asks into scoped Jira stories, writing boilerplate code and tests, producing documentation, rebuilding review decks, and context-switching across Confluence, Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD, and Vertex AI. New members took weeks to be productive because the derivatives domain and our data model lived in people's heads.
The problem we set out to solve was efficiency and business alignment simultaneously: strip out the low-value toil that wraps every project, while keeping the output tightly connected to the business ask. We standardized entirely on Gemini — Gemini CLI, the Gemini App, and Gems — so the assistance follows our existing workflow rather than forcing us to adopt a new one.
The Solution
The centerpiece is an orchestrated software lifecycle built on Gemini CLI plus MCP servers. From the terminal, the CLI drives a project end to end: it ingests a requirement, asks clarifying questions back to the analyst, drafts a technical design, reads and writes to Confluence over MCP, creates Jira stories, scaffolds the project folder structure and notebooks, generates the code, and produces test cases, unit tests, and functional tests — then sends status notifications by Gmail and Google Chat as each stage completes. What used to be a dozen manual handoffs across separate tools is now a single, observable flow the analyst supervises rather than executes by hand.
Two configuration layers make this reliable and consistent. We author GEMINI.md and SKILLS.md files that encode how our team does every software activity — requirements documents, design docs, Jira story format, code-review standards, documentation style, test-case and unit-test conventions, project folder structure, notebook scaffolding, and converting Python notebooks to .py modules for deployment. Because the conventions live in these files, every analyst gets the same standards-compliant output instead of ad-hoc prompting. On top of that, we use a Gem specifically for Jira: it takes a business ask and produces properly formatted epics, stories, and acceptance criteria, and lets the team quickly refine and re-shape tickets. The Gemini App handles the front-of-funnel and communication work — turning a stakeholder conversation into a structured problem statement, summarizing microstructure and digital-analytics findings, and drafting the narrative for our Google Slides business reviews.
Value to the Team / CME Group
Across a team of 7, the tracked savings total roughly 700 hours per quarter — about 16.5 hours saved per person per sprint, or ~28% of sprint capacity returned to higher-value work. The largest single gain is in software development, where automating scaffolding, code, and tests cut effort by ~75% (about 6.25 hours per person per sprint). Documentation and presentation prep each fell ~80%, quality assurance ~70%, requirements-and-Jira grooming ~50%, and email/communication ~60%. Those percentages compound because they hit tasks that recur every sprint.
The qualitative gains matter as much as the hours. Automated unit, functional, and regression tests raised code quality and caught defects earlier, so less time is lost to rework. With the mechanical parts of the lifecycle handled, the team spends far less time writing boilerplate and far more time on the actual business problems — understanding client navigation and trading behavior, sharpening microstructure and liquidity analysis, and collaborating directly with the derivatives business. And because every project now starts from a Gem-structured Jira story and moves through GEMINI.md/SKILLS.md-governed steps, deliverables map cleanly back to the original stakeholder question, reducing misalignment and rework cycles. New-hire ramp shortened as well, since the domain and engineering conventions now live in shared, executable configuration rather than in individual heads.
